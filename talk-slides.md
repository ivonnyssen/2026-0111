- You know everyone: who needs help, who is your best person

# The Weekly Review

- Critical projects only
- Owner, Last Update, Last Status, Today's Status
- Brevity: issues hide behind words
- Risks and owners, or it's all watermelons
- This is uncomfortable for you and for your teams

> Visual: watermelon.png
> Notes: ~1:30. The most concrete, reusable thing in the talk. The columns are deliberately boring; the discipline is in the comparison between last status and today's status. A project that has been green for weeks with no risks listed is the watermelon: green outside, red inside.

# Your Voice Becomes Very Quiet (512)

**Calendar, Common Sense, Tenets**

- What you spend time on will be good. What you ignore will blow up.
- Fight complexity with common sense
- Tenets keep your North Star when you are not there
- Tech, Business, Operations need different leadership.

**Language**
Repeat yourself. Nobody hears you the first time, or the tenth.

**Anecdotes**
Pick one defect that disagrees with your mental model and chase it down. 9 out of 10 reveal something important.

> Visual: figure-bound-gag.png (adds gag)
> Notes: ~3:00. Placed and order for the next day and could not add items. Where I should have been able to. Followed up with the team and they told me that is by design, when the pick window closes. But it was the previous day, so the explanation did not hold water. There was a bug involving time zones and the feature was inaccessible to customers. There was also no metric to show whether customers were using the feature, so we had built it, deployed it, claimed victory and it did not work - silently; it is the best material in the deck. Spoken: goal reviews become education sessions for you and your team. Speed is your job: experimentation, learning, delivery, decisions. The leaves of the org tree are still the most knowledgeable people: connect, travel, connect again. Your job is still systems engineering, focus on information flow.

# Tenets

## Bad Tenet

**We will deliver high quality software for our customers.**

## Good Tenet (Unless you know better ones)

- Sharp, Display Trade-Offs, Not a tautology

Example from Rusty Photon - my astrophotography app

**Make the best use of night time.** Clear, dark hours are the scarce resource; everything else (build time, code size, convenience) is negotiable against it.

# What Never Changes

- Be the Shepherd and the Wolf.
- Leadership is air cover.
- If you did not show your team how to win, they will make up their own goals.
- Your attention is a spotlight.
- Your future is not in tech, it is in business.

> Notes: ~1:30. Moved to the end so it works as the summary and the "one thing never changes" payoff from the opening.
> Spoken halves:
> - Shepherd and the Wolf: team performance depends on everyone knowing that everyone else is pulling their weight. You protect the flock and you remove the ones who are not.
> - Air cover: everyone will always think your team can do better. Your job is to absorb that so they can work.
> - How to win: if you did not show your team what winning looks like, they will make up their own goals and priorities, and they will be wrong in ways you cannot see.
> - Spotlight: where you focus your attention, things improve. The problems do not disappear, they migrate to wherever you are not looking.
> Dropped from slide, keep for Q&A: fire the "this cannot be done" people, connect your teams to your customers, expect failure and fail fast.
