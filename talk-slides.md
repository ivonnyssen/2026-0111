# Small Team (8)

**Your job is to say no.**

> Visual: figure-free.png
> Notes: ~2:30. Say no, for the most part.
> - You know everyone: who needs help, who is your best person.
> - Your value is judgment, not visibility. Full information means your value is deciding, not gathering.
> - Signal when you need help. Build the habit now, it cannot be added later.
> - Pick the right process and expect it to change under pressure. Process efficiency, what comes next, think outside the box.
> Figure: unimpeded, start of the four-step series.

# The Weekly Review

Owner · Last update · Last status · Today’s status

> Visual: watermelon.png
> Notes: ~1:30. The most concrete, reusable thing in the talk.
> - Critical projects only.
> - The columns are deliberately boring; the discipline is in the comparison between last status and today’s status.
> - Brevity: issues hide behind words.
> - Risks and owners, or it’s all watermelons. A project that has been green for weeks with no risks listed is the watermelon: green outside, red inside.
> - This is uncomfortable for you and for your teams.

# You Are Doomed (128)

You can steer 12 times a year.

“As of mm/dd we are COLOR, because …”

**Make your organization self-sufficient.**

> Visual: figure-bound.png
> Notes: ~2:00. Cadence is the point. You can steer 12 times a year, but your teams can be way more agile.
> - Perhaps one super-important project you still stay close to.
> - Formal goal review is your best tool: why is this important, how is this measured, as of date we are COLOR because.
> - What does winning look like?
> - Teaching is now part of your job description. Your only way to scale is to build your directs.
> - Your organization is better for your presence, but does not require it to function.

# Your Voice Gets Very Quiet (512)

**Calendar.**

**Language.**

**Anecdotes.**

> Visual: figure-bound-gag.png
> Notes: ~3:00.
> Calendar, common sense, tenets: what you spend time on will be good, what you ignore will blow up. Fight complexity with common sense. Tenets keep your North Star when you are not there. Tech, business and operations need different leadership.
> Language: repeat yourself. Nobody hears you the first time, or the tenth.
> Anecdotes: pick one defect that disagrees with your mental model and chase it down. 9 out of 10 reveal something important. Story: placed an order for the next day and could not add items, where I should have been able to. Followed up with the team and they told me that is by design, when the pick window closes. But it was the previous day, so the explanation did not hold water. There was a bug involving time zones and the feature was inaccessible to customers. There was also no metric to show whether customers were using the feature, so we had built it, deployed it, claimed victory and it did not work, silently.
> Spoken: goal reviews become education sessions for you and your team. Speed is your job: experimentation, learning, delivery, decisions. The leaves of the org tree are still the most knowledgeable people: connect, travel, connect again. Your job is still systems engineering, focus on information flow.
> Figure: adds the gag.

# Tenets

Bad tenet: **We will deliver high quality software for our customers.**

Good tenet (unless you know better ones): **Make the best use of night time.** (Rusty Photon, my astrophotography app)

> Notes: A good tenet is sharp, displays a trade-off, and is not a tautology. Night time: clear, dark hours are the scarce resource; everything else (build time, code size, convenience) is negotiable against it.
